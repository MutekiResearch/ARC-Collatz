\begin{abstract}
This note records a revised post-release adversarial audit of four structurally
important points in the Cobham-free Full General ARC proof.  The audit targets
(A1) transfer from base-$2$ finite-kernel automaticity to base-$3$ automaticity
of the odd subsequence, (A2) dyadic nowhere-thickness of the odd-subsequence
change set, (A3) the odd-base topological-pumping step from dyadic
nowhere-thickness to finite support, and (A4) the possibility that the main
rigidity theorem is vacuous or that the exclusion of zero is not genuinely
sharp.

All four audit items have now been reconstructed at the paper level and
encoded in separate Lean 4 audit files under an explicit independence scope.
A1 avoids the earlier packaged transfer result; A2 reuses the lower
local-density theorem but independently rebuilds the A2-specific affine
pullback; A3 directly avoids the packaged Stage 6--10 conclusions while
reusing lower infrastructure.  Lean kernel type-checking completed
successfully for every audit file, and each audit module also completed its
targeted \code{lake build}.  For the principal audit endpoints, \code{\#print axioms}
reported only the standard Lean/Mathlib dependencies \code{propext},
\code{Classical.choice}, and \code{Quot.sound}, with no \code{sorryAx}.
No counterexample or confirmed defect in the published Full General ARC theorem
was found.

The strongest result of the revised audit is therefore not merely a source
trace.  A1 is re-derived from the kernel definitions using the primitive-root
behavior of $2$ modulo powers of $3$; A2 is rebuilt from the lower local-density
theorem by an explicit affine pullback $x=2n+1$; A3 reconstructs the
odd-base pumping-to-dyadic-filling contradiction; and A4 gives an explicit
finite-kernel shortcut-invariant witness in every base $B\ge2$ whose value at
zero may differ from its positive value.

Here ``independent'' means independent of the corresponding packaged proof
endpoint and reconstructed through lower-level ingredients.  It does not mean
external third-party peer review, nor does it claim complete import-graph
isolation of every lower lemma reused by the audit files.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status and purpose}

The parent paper proves the following rigidity statement for the shortcut
Collatz map
\[
T(n)=
\begin{cases}
 n/2, & n\equiv0\pmod 2,\\[2mm]
 (3n+1)/2, & n\equiv1\pmod 2.
\end{cases}
\]
For every base $B\ge2$, if $a:\Nzero\to\alpha$ has finite base-$B$ kernel and
satisfies
\[
a(T(n))=a(n)\qquad(n\in\Nzero),

independent.

mathematical or literature review could ever be useful.

\section{Audit A1: independent base-$3$ kernel transfer}

Let $a:\Nzero\to\alpha$ satisfy the two shortcut branch identities
\[
a(2n)=a(n),\qquad a(2n+1)=a(3n+2),
\]
and define
\[
b(n)=a(2n+1).
\]
The target is
\[
\K_2(a)\text{ finite}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\K_3(b)\text{ finite}.
\]
The revised audit re-derives this implication without calling the earlier
packaged ARC2 transfer theorem.

\subsection{Independent paper reconstruction}

Take an arbitrary base-$3$ kernel residual of $b$,
\[
v(n)=b(3^e n+r),\qquad 0\le r<3^e.
\]
Using the odd branch once,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v(n)
 &=a\bigl(2(3^e n+r)+1\bigr)\\
 &=a\bigl(3^{e+1}n+(3r+2)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Set $j=e+1$ and $c=3r+2$.  Then
\[
0<c<3^j,\qquad 3\nmid c.
\]
Because $2$ is a primitive root modulo $3^j$, choose $s\ge0$ such that
\[
2^s c\equiv-1\pmod{3^j}.
\]
Hence
\[
2^s c+1=q3^j
\]
for some integer $q\ge1$.  Repeated use of the even identity together with
the odd identity in reverse gives
\[
a(3^j n+c)=a\bigl(2^{s+j}n+R\bigr),
\qquad R=2^j q-1.
\]
The same arithmetic gives
\[
0\le R<2^{s+j}.
\]
Therefore every base-$3$ residual $v$ of $b$ is literally a base-$2$ kernel
residual of $a$.  Thus
\[
\K_3(b)\subseteq\K_2(a),
\]
and the finiteness of $\K_2(a)$ implies the finiteness of $\K_3(b)$.
No Cobham theorem, Collatz convergence statement, or finite-alphabet
hypothesis is used in this reconstruction.

\section{Audit A2: independent dyadic thinness reconstruction}

Define the odd subsequence and its adjacent-change set by
\[
b(n)=a(2n+1),\qquad
\D_b=\{n\in\Nzero:b(n)\ne b(n+1)\}.
\]
The concern is the interaction between shortcut-Collatz arithmetic and dyadic
residue classes.  The revised audit isolates exactly the A2-specific affine
step and rebuilds it from the lower local-density theorem.

\subsection{Independent affine pullback}

Start with an arbitrary dyadic parent class
\[
n\equiv r\pmod{2^m}.
\]
The affine map
\[
x=2n+1
\]
sends this parent into
\[
x\equiv2r+1\pmod{2^{m+1}}.
\]
Apply the already established base-independent local-density theorem to this
odd affine image with gap $H=2$.  It supplies a deeper integer dyadic child on
which
\[
a(x)=a(x+2).
\]
The audit then constructs explicitly a deeper child in the original
$n$-variable whose entire affine image lies in that local-density child.  On
that child,
\[
a(2n+1)=a(2n+3),
\]
so
\[
b(n)=b(n+1).
\]
After converting the integer cylinder to a natural residue class, the child is
nonempty, remains inside the prescribed parent, and is disjoint from $\D_b$.
This is exactly dyadic nowhere-thickness.

\section{Audit A3: independent odd-base topological-pumping reconstruction}

Let $q\ge3$ be odd and let $s:\Nzero\to\{0,1\}$ have finite base-$q$ kernel.
The target statement is that dyadic nowhere-thickness of the support forces the
support to be finite.  This is the most structurally delicate mixed-base step:
the automaton is organized by powers of $q$, while thinness is organized by
powers of $2$.

\subsection{Independent paper reconstruction}

Assume the support is infinite.  A finite MSD automaton then has a positive
canonical support word longer than its number of states.  Pumping yields a
nonempty loop block.  Doubling that loop gives a pumped numerical family
\[
N_k=N_0+dG_k,
\qquad
G_k=1+Q+\cdots+Q^{k-1},
\qquad Q=q^{2L},
\]
where $L>0$ is the loop length.  Positivity gives $d>0$, so write
\[
d=2^s(2v+1).
\]
Since $q$ is odd,
\[
Q=q^{2L}\equiv1\pmod4.
\]
If
\[
j-i=2^t(2u+1),
\]
the doubled-loop geometric-sum arithmetic gives an exact dyadic displacement
\[
N_j-N_i=2^{s+t}(2z+1).
\]
Consequently, for $0\le i<j<2^M$, the normalized pump residues are distinct
modulo $2^M$: the valuation $t$ of $j-i$ is always $<M$.  There are exactly
$2^M$ indices and $2^M$ residue classes, so the normalized pump map is a
permutation of all residues modulo $2^M$.

Restoring the factor $2^s$ and the offset $N_0$, the pumped support family hits
every deeper dyadic child of one parent cylinder.  Dyadic nowhere-thickness
requires an empty positive deeper child inside every parent cylinder.  The two
statements contradict each other.  Hence an infinite odd-base automatic support
cannot be dyadically nowhere thick, and the support must be finite.

\section{Audit A4: constructive non-vacuity and sharpness at zero}

The final audit item asks whether the theorem could be true only because its
hypotheses describe an empty class, and whether excluding zero from the
rigidity conclusion is genuinely necessary.

For arbitrary values $x,y\in\alpha$, define
\[
a(0)=x,\qquad a(n)=y\quad(n>0).
\]
For every base $B\ge2$, every base-$B$ kernel residual of this sequence is
either the original sequence or the constant sequence $n\mapsto y$.  Hence
\[
|\K_B(a)|\le2.
\]
Also $T(0)=0$ and $T(n)>0$ for every $n>0$, so
\[
a(T(n))=a(n)\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
If $x\ne y$, then $a(0)\ne a(1)$, while all positive values are equal.  Thus
the hypothesis class is nonempty in every base and the freedom at zero is
real.

\section{What a genuine counterexample would have to provide}

To refute the Full General ARC theorem, it is not enough to propose a coloring
that is constant on Collatz orbits, or a sparse automatic set, or a coloring
that respects only the even branch.  A genuine counterexample must explicitly
exhibit some $B\ge2$ and a sequence $a:\Nzero\to\alpha$ satisfying all three
conditions simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the full base-$B$ kernel $\K_B(a)$ is finite;
\item $a(T(n))=a(n)$ for every $n\ge0$;
\item there exist positive $m,n$ with $a(m)\ne a(n)$.
\end{enumerate}
For example, assigning colors to hypothetical nonstandard Collatz components
might satisfy (ii), but it says nothing by itself about the finite-kernel
condition (i).  Likewise, extending a coloring by $a(2^kq)=a(q)$ enforces the
even relation but does not automatically enforce the odd relation
\[
a(2n+1)=a(3n+2).
\]
Any claimed counterexample must survive these exact checks.
